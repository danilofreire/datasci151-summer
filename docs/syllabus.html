Syllabus

Course description

Welcome to DATASCI 151! This Maymester course introduces data analysis and statistical computing with Python and SQL. It is designed for beginners, so you do not need any programming experience to take it.

We will cover the basics of Python programming, Jupyter Notebooks for reproducible research, data wrangling and merging with SQL, and data visualisation.

You will work with real datasets and problems throughout the course and use these tools to answer questions with data. The course builds your technical skills and your critical thinking, which you will need for any data analysis you do later on.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  1. Write Python functions and do mathematical operations
  2. Clean and manipulate data with Python libraries such as pandas
  3. Merge and manage databases with SQL
  4. Create visualisations to communicate what you find in the data
  5. Use Jupyter Notebooks for reproducible research
  6. Solve data analysis and statistical computing problems on your own

Prerequisites

None! Everyone is welcome, whatever your experience with programming or data analysis. If you are not sure whether the course is right for you, just send me an email and I will be happy to help.

Course information

We will meet online, Monday to Friday, from 11:00am to 12:20pm EDT. Please review the materials before each class. You can find all course information in our GitHub repository at https://github.com/danilofreire/datasci151-summer.

I plan to follow the schedule, but I also want to adapt it to your pace and learning style, so the syllabus may change during the course. I will announce any changes in class and on Canvas. Please also check the course repository regularly for updates.

Software

We will mainly use Python, a free, general-purpose programming language that is widely used in data science and machine learning. I recommend the Anaconda distribution, which already includes many of the libraries we need, such as pandas, NumPy and Jupyter.

You can use any text editor you like, but I recommend VS Code with the Python extension. PyCharm is another excellent option. Feeling adventurous? Try Neovim with the coc-pyright plugin (that is, if you can exit the editor). :)

We will also use Jupyter Notebooks in class. Jupyter comes with Anaconda, but please also install the Jupyter extension for VS Code. We will have a hands-on session on how to use it.

I have written a series of tutorials to help you install Anaconda, VS Code and SQLite, use Jupyter Notebooks and conda environments, and open a free GitHub educational account. Please work through them as soon as possible, so you have all the tools ready when we start.

Artificial intelligence

You may use AI tools for the assignments, as long as they support your own thinking and do not replace it. AI tools are not allowed during quizzes, because quizzes are individual tests of your understanding. If you use AI for an assignment, please check the output carefully, as you are responsible for any errors in what you submit. Cite all your sources, including AI tools, and add a short note at the end of any document where you used AI.

Accessibility services

I want every student to succeed, and I am happy to provide accommodations. If you have a medical or health condition that may affect your work, please visit the Department of Accessibility Services (DAS) to learn about the accommodations available. Please send me your DAS Accommodation Letter at the start of the course, or as soon as you receive it.

DAS accommodations such as extra time apply only to quizzes. They do not apply to assignments, because I release assignments in advance and you can work on them at your own pace. If you are an athlete or have other commitments, please tell me about any scheduling conflicts early in the course. I will do my best to accommodate you, although I cannot promise to meet every request. Any questions? Just let me know.

Assignments and grading

Assignments (50%). You will complete five assignments during the course. They give you hands-on practice with the lecture material, and each one has conceptual questions and practical exercises. Please complete them individually and acknowledge all your sources, including textbooks, articles and AI tools.

Late submissions automatically receive half credit. Please submit your assignments on Canvas as HTML or PDF files by midnight on the due date. These formats are easier for me to read than notebooks.

Quizzes (50%). You will take three in-class quizzes, all on Fridays. Each quiz covers that week’s lectures and asks you to apply the concepts to new problems. Quizzes are open-book and open-notes, but AI tools are not allowed. They are also individual, so please do not discuss the questions with your classmates.
